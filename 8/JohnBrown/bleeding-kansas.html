1855–1856 • Kansas Territory

Bleeding Kansas

Before Harpers Ferry, John Brown became nationally known in Kansas, where proslavery and antislavery settlers fought over the territory's future.

Kansas became a preview of the Civil War: Americans were already fighting and killing one another over slavery years before Fort Sumter.

Why Kansas Exploded

The Kansas-Nebraska Act used popular sovereignty to let settlers decide whether Kansas would allow slavery. Antislavery settlers moved in hoping to make Kansas free. Proslavery settlers and armed men from Missouri—often called Border Ruffians—tried to push it the other way.

Brown traveled to Kansas in 1855 to join several of his sons. By the spring of 1856, political arguments had become armed conflict.

How the Violence Escalated

A proslavery force entered the antislavery town of Lawrence, destroyed newspaper presses, looted property, and burned the Free State Hotel. The attack enraged Brown.
Brown and a small group seized five proslavery men from their homes near Pottawatomie Creek and killed them with broadswords. Brown's exact physical role in each killing has long been debated, but he led the party and accepted responsibility for the action.
Brown's force fought a proslavery group led by Henry Clay Pate. After several hours, Pate and many of his men surrendered.
Hundreds of proslavery fighters attacked the free-state town of Osawatomie. Brown led a smaller defense before being forced to retreat. His son Frederick was killed.
Did You Know? The fighting at Black Jack has sometimes been described as one of the first battles of the coming Civil War because organized armed groups were already fighting over slavery in 1856.

The Pottawatomie Problem

Brown believed the attack on Lawrence proved that proslavery forces would use violence unless antislavery settlers struck back. His response at Pottawatomie remains the most troubling part of his Kansas story.

The five men killed were proslavery settlers, but they were not killed during a battle. Brown's party took them from their homes at night and killed them. Supporters later argued that Brown was answering a campaign of intimidation and violence. Critics called the killings murder.

This is one reason Brown's legacy cannot be reduced to a simple hero-or-villain story.

Big Question

Does fighting for a just cause make every method used in that fight just?
