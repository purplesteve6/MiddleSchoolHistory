October 16–18, 1859

The Raid on Harpers Ferry

Brown planned a dramatic strike against slavery: seize federal weapons, arm an expanding liberation force, and use the mountains as a base for future action.

The raid failed militarily in only about 36 hours—but its political impact lasted far longer.

The Plan

Why Harpers Ferry?

The town contained a major U.S. armory and arsenal. Brown hoped to seize weapons and use the nearby Blue Ridge Mountains for a long campaign against slavery.

Kennedy Farm

Brown and his followers secretly gathered at a Maryland farmhouse a few miles from Harpers Ferry. Weapons—including hundreds of pikes—were hidden there while the men studied maps and waited.

36 Hours at Harpers Ferry

Brown and 18 men move toward Harpers Ferry. Other members remain behind in support roles.
Raiders take the bridges, U.S. Armory and Arsenal, and the Rifle Works. The operation initially succeeds with little resistance.
A Baltimore & Ohio train is stopped. Railroad worker Heyward Shepherd, a free Black man, is shot and mortally wounded. News of the raid begins spreading.
Townspeople and militia fire on the raiders. Brown's group becomes trapped. Several raiders are killed, including Dangerfield Newby.
U.S. Marines commanded by Army Colonel Robert E. Lee arrive. Lieutenant J.E.B. Stuart offers surrender terms. Brown refuses. Marines storm the engine house and capture him.

Who Joined Brown?

Brown's force was interracial and included both free and formerly enslaved Black men. Twenty-one men gathered for the operation, although only 19 entered Harpers Ferry with Brown that night.

Dangerfield Newby
A formerly enslaved man hoping to free his wife and children. Killed during the raid.
John A. Copeland Jr.
A free Black abolitionist from Oberlin, Ohio. Captured and later executed.
Shields Green
Formerly enslaved; chose to join Brown after the Chambersburg meeting. Captured and executed.
Watson & Oliver Brown
Two of John Brown's sons. Both died from wounds received during the raid.
Did You Know? Brown's last stand took place in the Armory's small fire engine house. The building later became known as John Brown's Fort.

Why Did It Fail?

Too SlowBrown did not immediately leave town with weapons.
Local ResistanceTownspeople and militia quickly blocked escape routes.
No Mass UprisingThe large wave of enslaved recruits Brown hoped for did not appear during the short raid.

By the morning of October 18, the raid was over. Ten of Brown's men had been killed, five escaped, and Brown and several others were captured.

Turning Point

If the raid failed so quickly, why did it frighten the South and inspire some people in the North?
