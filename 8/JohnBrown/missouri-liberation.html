December 1858–March 1859

The Missouri Liberation Raid

Brown's most successful direct action against slavery began with an armed raid into Missouri and ended with a long journey toward freedom in Canada.

Brown did not only argue against slavery. In Missouri he deliberately entered slave territory, liberated enslaved people, and helped move them beyond the reach of U.S. slavery.

Across the Border

On the night of December 20, 1858, Brown led one of two small parties from Kansas into Missouri. Together they liberated 11 enslaved people. During the operation, a slaveholder named David Cruise was killed by a member of the other party.

Missouri authorities demanded Brown's arrest and the return of the people who had escaped. Even some antislavery allies criticized the raid because they feared violent retaliation.

Was This the Underground Railroad?

What was familiar

The group relied on secret shelter, trusted antislavery allies, and routes that helped freedom seekers move north. Those were all features of the Underground Railroad.

What was different

Brown did not simply assist people who had already escaped. He crossed into a slave state with armed men and deliberately removed enslaved people from slavery. It was an organized liberation raid.

A Success—With a Warning

Unlike Harpers Ferry, this operation accomplished its immediate goal: the liberated people escaped slavery and ultimately reached Canada. But the raid also revealed Brown's growing belief that direct armed action was the most effective way to attack slavery.

Within months, he would attempt something vastly larger.
