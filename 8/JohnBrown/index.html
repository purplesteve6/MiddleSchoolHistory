John Brown

John Brown believed slavery was a terrible moral wrong—and eventually decided that words and politics were not enough to destroy it. He helped enslaved people reach freedom, fought proslavery forces in Kansas, and led an armed raid on Harpers Ferry that shocked the nation.

Brown's story forces a difficult question: when people are fighting an unjust system, can violence ever be justified?

Before the Famous Beard

Brown grew up in a deeply religious family that opposed slavery. As an adult he worked at many businesses—tanning, farming, wool dealing, and land speculation—but his antislavery beliefs became the central cause of his life.

He came to believe that slavery would not disappear through speeches or elections alone. Brown became a abolitionist willing to use force. That made him different from many antislavery activists who insisted on peaceful methods.

In the 1840s Brown also became connected with Black abolitionists and communities of formerly enslaved people. He later lived for a time in North Elba, New York, near a settlement created to help Black families own land.
