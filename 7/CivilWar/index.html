The Civil War

For four years, Americans fought Americans in the deadliest conflict in United States history. Eleven Southern states tried to leave the United States and form a new nation. The United States fought to preserve the Union. At the center of the crisis was slavery—especially whether a nation founded on liberty would continue protecting and expanding it.

The Civil War began as a fight over secession and the survival of the Union, but it became a war that also destroyed slavery in the United States.

How Did the United States Get Here?

The Civil War did not suddenly appear in 1861. For decades, Americans argued over slavery, political power, westward expansion, and the balance between state and federal authority.

By the 1850s, each attempt to settle the slavery question seemed to create a new crisis. Violence in Kansas, the Supreme Court's Dred Scott decision, John Brown's raid on Harpers Ferry, Abraham Lincoln's election, and Southern secession all made compromise harder. When Confederate forces fired on Fort Sumter in April 1861, the political crisis became a shooting war.

Two Sides

The Union

Also called: the United States or the North

The Union included the free states plus several slaveholding border states that did not secede. Its government rejected the idea that states could simply leave the United States.

The Confederacy

Also called: the South

Eleven slave states seceded and formed the Confederate States of America. Confederate leaders sought independence and built their new government around the protection of slavery.
