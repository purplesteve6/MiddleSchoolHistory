Before the Fighting

The Union navy was blockading the Confederate coast. At Norfolk, the Confederacy rebuilt the burned U.S. steam frigate Merrimack as an armored warship named CSS Virginia. On March 8, Virginia steamed into Hampton Roads and smashed wooden Union warships. Overnight, a newly arrived Union ironclad—the USS Monitor—took position to stop it.

This page is really about technology as much as tactics. The battle did not decide the Civil War, but it revealed that iron armor, steam engines, and new ship design were making old-style wooden fleets obsolete.

Person to Know: John L. Worden

Worden commanded the Monitor in its famous duel. He represented the new kind of naval officer the age of ironclads was creating.

How It Unfolded

1

Virginia Attacks the Wooden Fleet

On March 8, CSS Virginia sank USS Cumberland, forced USS Congress to surrender, and threatened the rest of the Union fleet.

2

Monitor Arrives

USS Monitor looked nothing like a traditional warship. It rode low in the water and carried two large guns inside a rotating iron turret.

3

Iron Meets Iron

On March 9, Monitor and Virginia pounded one another for hours. Neither ship could seriously damage the other, and the duel ended without a clear winner.

People called the Monitor a “cheesebox on a raft” because its low deck and round turret looked so unusual.

Politics & the War

Why this battle mattered beyond the battlefield

Hampton Roads connected directly to the Union blockade, part of the broader strategy often called the Anaconda Plan. If the Union could shut Confederate ports and dominate the coast, it would squeeze the Confederacy economically and militarily.

A battle can be a draw on the battlefield and still be a turning point in technology.

Why It Mattered

The battle itself was inconclusive, but the meaning of the technology was clear. Navies around the world now knew that armored steam warships were the future and wooden battle fleets were becoming outdated.

Think 3 M’s: Monitor, Metal, Modern Navy. Hampton Roads made the modern navy impossible to ignore.
