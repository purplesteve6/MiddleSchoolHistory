Vicksburg

ResultUnion victory

Before the Fighting

Vicksburg sat high above a sharp bend in the Mississippi River. Confederate guns there could threaten Union shipping, and the city helped connect the eastern Confederacy with Arkansas, Louisiana, and Texas. The Union had captured New Orleans and other river positions, but Vicksburg still blocked complete control of the Mississippi.

How It Unfolded

1

Grant Goes South

After several failed approaches, Grant marched his army down the west side of the Mississippi while Admiral David Porter ran gunboats past Vicksburg’s batteries at night.

2

Cross, Move Inland, Win Battles

Grant crossed the river south of Vicksburg, moved inland, captured Jackson, Mississippi, and defeated Confederate forces at Champion Hill and the Big Black River.

3

The Siege

After direct assaults failed, Grant surrounded Vicksburg. Soldiers and civilians endured weeks of bombardment and shortages until Pemberton surrendered on July 4.

How do you capture a fortress on high bluffs?

Vicksburg’s guns dominate the river and earlier Union approaches have failed.

Problem: Direct approaches had already shown how difficult the terrain and defenses were.
Grant’s final approach: March south, cross the Mississippi below Vicksburg, cut loose from a fixed supply base, win battles inland, and approach the city from the east.
Problem: Leaving Vicksburg in Confederate hands would keep the Mississippi River divided and preserve a major Confederate supply connection.

Why It Mattered

The fall of Vicksburg gave the Union control of the Mississippi River and cut the Confederacy in two. It also made Ulysses S. Grant the most successful Union general of the war so far. Lincoln soon brought Grant eastward, where he would eventually take command of all Union armies.

Vicksburg was less about one dramatic charge than about geography, movement, supply lines, and patience.
