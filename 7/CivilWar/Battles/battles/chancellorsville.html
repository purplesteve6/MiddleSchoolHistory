April 30–May 6, 1863 • Spotsylvania County, Virginia

Chancellorsville

A daring Confederate attack wins a famous victory at a terrible cost.

Confederate victory
1863LEE TAKES A RISKSpotsylvania County, Virginia

Battle at a Glance

DateApril 30–May 6, 1863
LocationSpotsylvania County, Virginia
CommandersJoseph Hooker • Robert E. Lee & Thomas “Stonewall” Jackson
ResultConfederate victory

Before the Fighting

Union General Joseph Hooker crossed rivers above Fredericksburg and threatened Lee from the west while another Union force remained near Fredericksburg. Hooker had more soldiers, but Lee chose an extremely risky response: divide his smaller army in the face of the enemy and attack.

How It Unfolded

1

Lee Divides His Army

Lee left part of his force to watch Fredericksburg and moved west with most of his army to confront Hooker.

2

Jackson’s Flank March

Stonewall Jackson led about 28,000 men on a long hidden march around the Union army. Late on May 2, they smashed into the exposed Union right flank.

3

Victory—and Loss

The Union army retreated, giving Lee one of his most impressive victories. But Jackson was accidentally shot by Confederate soldiers while scouting after dark. He died eight days later.

A Victory the Confederacy Could Not Fully Celebrate

Battlefield resultLee defeated a larger Union army by dividing his own force and attacking aggressively.
The costStonewall Jackson was hit by friendly fire after dark, lost his left arm, developed pneumonia, and died on May 10.

Lee reportedly called Jackson’s death a loss to the whole army. The victory strengthened Confederate confidence, but Lee would begin the Gettysburg Campaign without the commander who had led many of his most successful flank attacks.

Why It Mattered

Chancellorsville is remembered as a masterpiece of aggressive battlefield command. Yet the Confederacy could not easily replace Stonewall Jackson. Lee won the battle while losing one of his most important lieutenants—just weeks before he began a new invasion of the North.

Chancellorsville was one of Lee’s greatest victories, but the Confederacy paid for it with Stonewall Jackson.
