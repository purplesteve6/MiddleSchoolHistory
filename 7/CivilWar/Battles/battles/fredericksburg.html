Before the Fighting

Union General Ambrose Burnside hoped to move quickly toward Richmond by crossing the Rappahannock River at Fredericksburg. Delays in receiving pontoon bridges gave Lee time to occupy strong high ground west of town. Once the Union army crossed the river, it faced Confederate defenders on ridges and behind a stone wall at Marye’s Heights.

Fredericksburg is one of the clearest examples of how hard it was to attack a strong defensive position in the Civil War, especially when rifled weapons and artillery could tear up advancing infantry.

Person to Know: Ambrose Burnside

Burnside’s name became unforgettable for another reason: his dramatic facial hair style eventually gave English the word “sideburns.”

How It Unfolded

1

Crossing Under Fire

Union engineers built pontoon bridges while Confederate sharpshooters fired from town. Union troops eventually crossed and occupied Fredericksburg.

2

Assaults on Marye’s Heights

On December 13, wave after wave of Union soldiers advanced across open ground toward the stone wall at the base of Marye’s Heights.

3

Lee’s Line Holds

The attacks failed with terrible losses. Burnside could not break through, and the Union army eventually withdrew.

During the battle Lee is associated with the famous line, “It is well that war is so terrible, otherwise we should grow too fond of it.”

Politics & the War

Why this battle mattered beyond the battlefield

The defeat hurt Burnside badly. Soon afterward he was replaced by Joseph Hooker, showing students that military setbacks often led directly to political pressure and changes in leadership.

Fredericksburg is a warning that bravery by itself cannot overcome bad ground and strong defenses.

Why It Mattered

Fredericksburg was a crushing Union defeat. It strengthened Lee’s reputation while damaging Burnside’s. It also deepened Northern frustration, because the Army of the Potomac had suffered huge losses for very little gain.

Marye’s Heights = a wall of fire. Picture the stone wall to remember why the attacks failed.
