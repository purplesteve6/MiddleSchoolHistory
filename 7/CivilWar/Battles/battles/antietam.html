Before the Fighting

After victories in Virginia, Robert E. Lee moved into Maryland. He hoped a successful invasion might weaken Northern support for the war and perhaps encourage foreign recognition of the Confederacy. Union soldiers found a lost copy of Lee’s orders, giving George McClellan unusual insight into Confederate movements.

Antietam matters militarily, but it also matters politically. This is the battle that connects the war for union with the war against slavery most directly.

Person to Know: Clara Barton

Clara Barton brought supplies and aid to Antietam and later became one of the most famous nurses of the Civil War. Her work reminds students that battlefields were shaped by more than generals alone.

How It Unfolded

1

Morning: Cornfield and Dunker Church

Union and Confederate attacks swept back and forth across a cornfield and around the small Dunker Church. Units were shattered in repeated assaults.

2

Midday: The Sunken Road

Fighting shifted to a farm lane later called the Bloody Lane. Union troops eventually broke the position, but Lee’s army survived.

3

Afternoon: Burnside Bridge

Union troops crossed Antietam Creek and threatened Lee’s right. Confederate reinforcements from Harpers Ferry arrived in time to stop the advance.

Photographer Alexander Gardner took shocking images of Antietam’s dead. Many Northern civilians had never seen the grim reality of war displayed so directly.

Politics & the War

Why this battle mattered beyond the battlefield

The Emancipation Proclamation did not instantly free every enslaved person in the United States, but it changed the war. It made the destruction of slavery in the rebellious states an official Union goal and opened the door for Black soldiers to serve the Union cause in large numbers.

“A fit and necessary war measure” is how Lincoln described emancipation in the war context. Antietam gave him the moment he needed.

Short context: Antietam created the political opening for Lincoln’s emancipation policy.

Why It Mattered

Lee withdrew to Virginia, allowing Lincoln to claim the result as a Union success. Days later Lincoln issued the preliminary Emancipation Proclamation, announcing that enslaved people in rebelling states would be declared free if those states did not return to the Union.

A for Antietam, A for Abolition’s opening. Antietam opened the way for emancipation as policy.
