Before the Fighting

In March 1864, Lincoln promoted Ulysses S. Grant to lieutenant general and placed him in command of all Union armies. Grant made his headquarters with George Meade’s Army of the Potomac. Instead of focusing mainly on capturing Richmond, Grant told Meade that Lee’s army itself was the main objective: wherever Lee went, the Union army would follow.

How It Unfolded

1

The Wilderness

On May 5–6, the armies fought in dense woods where smoke, brush, and fires made the battle chaotic. Neither side won a clear victory, and Union casualties were severe.

2

The Moment Soldiers Noticed

Previous Union armies had often pulled back after bloody fights with Lee. Grant instead ordered his army south toward Spotsylvania. Soldiers realized the campaign was continuing.

3

Spotsylvania & the Bloody Angle

At Spotsylvania, both armies built earthworks. On May 12, fighting around the Mule Shoe and Bloody Angle continued for nearly 20 hours. Grant eventually shifted south again rather than retreat north.

What Changed in 1864?

Earlier patternFight Lee → suffer heavy losses → pull back or pause

Several earlier Union commanders allowed Lee time to recover after major battles.

→
Grant’s patternFight Lee → shift south → fight again

Grant accepted that the Union would also suffer terrible losses, but he believed Lee could replace men and supplies less easily.

Attrition is not exactly “total war.”

Attrition here means keeping pressure on Lee’s army until Confederate resources are exhausted. Sherman’s later campaigns added a different idea: destroying railroads, crops, warehouses, and other resources that supported the Confederate war effort.

Why It Mattered

The Wilderness and Spotsylvania were not clean battlefield victories. Their importance was strategic. Grant kept Lee under continuous pressure and forced the Confederacy to replace losses it could not afford. The campaign became a brutal contest of endurance, resources, and manpower.

The key change in 1864 was not that Grant always won each battle—it was that after fighting Lee, he kept moving south.
