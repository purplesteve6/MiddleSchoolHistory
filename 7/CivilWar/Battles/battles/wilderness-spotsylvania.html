Before the Fighting

In 1864, Lincoln placed Ulysses S. Grant in charge of all Union armies. Grant traveled east and worked alongside George G. Meade against Lee’s Army of Northern Virginia. Unlike earlier Union commanders, Grant did not see one defeat or bloody fight as a signal to quit the campaign.

This page is less about one dramatic moment and more about a shift in strategy. Grant understood that the Union’s larger population and industry could matter most if he kept constant pressure on Lee.

Person to Know: Ulysses S. Grant

Grant’s key difference in 1864 was not that he avoided casualties—it was that he refused to break off the campaign after them. That persistence is central to understanding why these battles matter.

How It Unfolded

1

The Wilderness

Fighting erupted in thick woods where smoke, brush, and fire made control difficult. The battle ended without a clear tactical winner.

2

Grant Moves South Instead of Back

Instead of retreating north after the Wilderness, Grant marched south toward Spotsylvania. Union soldiers recognized that something had changed.

3

Spotsylvania and the Bloody Angle

At Spotsylvania, both sides dug in. Brutal fighting at the “Bloody Angle” showed how grinding and relentless the campaign had become.

In the Wilderness, brush fires ignited by battle trapped some wounded soldiers between the lines—one of the most horrifying episodes of the war.

Politics & the War

Why this battle mattered beyond the battlefield

Lincoln needed military success in 1864 because he faced reelection during wartime. Grant’s willingness to keep pressing Lee helped convince many Northerners that the Union was finally pursuing a strategy that matched its resources.

Attrition means wearing down an enemy through steady losses. Wilderness and Spotsylvania are the clearest middle-school example of that strategy.

Why It Mattered

Grant suffered terrible losses, but Lee could replace his men less easily. That is why historians talk about attrition here: the Union kept wearing down Confederate strength through continuous pressure.

W & S = Wear down and Southward. Grant wore Lee down and kept moving south.
