April 6–7, 1862 • Pittsburg Landing, Tennessee

Shiloh

Two days of fighting reveal how destructive the war can become.

Union victory
1862A BLOODY SHOCKPittsburg Landing, Tennessee

Battle at a Glance

DateApril 6–7, 1862
LocationPittsburg Landing, Tennessee
CommandersUlysses S. Grant & Don Carlos Buell • Albert Sidney Johnston & P. G. T. Beauregard
ResultUnion victory

Before the Fighting

Union victories at Forts Henry and Donelson had opened Tennessee to invasion. Ulysses S. Grant gathered his army near Pittsburg Landing while waiting for Don Carlos Buell to join him. Confederate General Albert Sidney Johnston decided to strike before the two Union armies could combine.

How It Unfolded

1

A Surprise Attack

At dawn on April 6, Confederate troops surged out of the woods and hit Union camps. Grant’s army was pushed back toward the Tennessee River.

2

The Union Hangs On

Fierce fighting raged at places later known as the Hornet’s Nest and the Peach Orchard. Johnston was mortally wounded, and Beauregard took command of the Confederate army.

3

Reinforcements Change the Fight

Buell’s troops arrived overnight. On April 7, the enlarged Union force counterattacked and drove the Confederates back toward Corinth.

Shiloh produced more than 23,000 casualties in two days. Americans who had imagined a short war were forced to confront what huge volunteer armies and modern weapons could do to one another.

Why It Mattered

Nearly 24,000 soldiers were killed, wounded, or missing over two days—an appalling number at the time. Shiloh showed that the western war would be just as important and deadly as the fighting in Virginia. Grant also emerged as a commander willing to keep fighting after a terrible first day.

Shiloh made it harder to believe that courage alone could make the war quick or clean.
