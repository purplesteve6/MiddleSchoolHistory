Before the Fighting

Union victories at Forts Henry and Donelson had opened Tennessee to invasion. Ulysses S. Grant gathered his army near Pittsburg Landing while waiting for Don Carlos Buell to arrive. Confederate General Albert Sidney Johnston decided to attack before the Union armies could combine.

Shiloh is an important middle-school battle because it helps students feel the war changing. Earlier fighting had been dramatic, but Shiloh made the scale of Civil War death much harder to ignore.

Person to Know: Ulysses S. Grant

Grant had been surprised, but he did not collapse. His willingness to keep fighting after setbacks became one of the qualities that eventually brought him to the top of Union command.

How It Unfolded

1

A Surprise Attack

At dawn on April 6, Confederate troops surged out of the woods and hit Union camps. Grant’s army was pushed back toward the Tennessee River.

2

The Union Holds On

Fierce fighting raged at places later known as the Hornet’s Nest and the Peach Orchard. Johnston was mortally wounded, and Beauregard took command.

3

Reinforcements Reverse the Battle

Buell’s troops arrived overnight. On April 7, the enlarged Union force counterattacked and drove the Confederates back toward Corinth.

The battlefield took its name from a small country church called Shiloh. Soldiers later gave other unforgettable names to parts of the battlefield, including the “Hornet’s Nest.”

Politics & the War

Why this battle mattered beyond the battlefield

Some Northern newspapers attacked Grant after the first day’s surprise. Lincoln did not dump him, however. He famously defended Grant with words often paraphrased as, “I can’t spare this man. He fights.”

Shiloh showed that a “victory” could still feel horrifying when the human cost was so high.

Why It Mattered

Nearly 24,000 soldiers were killed, wounded, captured, or missing over two days—an appalling number for Americans in 1862. Shiloh showed that the western war would be just as important and bloody as the fighting in Virginia.

Shiloh = Shock. The battle shocked Americans with the scale of the casualties.
