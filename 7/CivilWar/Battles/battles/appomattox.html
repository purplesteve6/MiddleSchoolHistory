Before the Fighting

By spring 1865, Confederate armies were collapsing under relentless pressure. Richmond and Petersburg had fallen, and Lee tried to lead the Army of Northern Virginia westward in hopes of finding supplies and perhaps joining other Confederate forces. Union troops moved quickly to block the escape.

Appomattox is often taught as “the end of the war,” though some Confederate forces surrendered later. It is still the clearest symbolic ending because Lee commanded the Confederacy’s most famous army.

Person to Know: Robert E. Lee

Lee’s surrender had enormous symbolic weight because the Army of Northern Virginia had become the Confederacy’s most famous field army.

How It Unfolded

1

The Road Is Cut Off

Union forces repeatedly blocked Lee’s routes and captured or scattered parts of his army.

2

One Last Realization

On April 9, Lee recognized that further fighting would be hopeless. Continuing would only cause more suffering without changing the result.

3

Grant and Lee Meet

The two generals met in the McLean House, where Lee surrendered the Army of Northern Virginia on generous terms.

Lee arrived in a handsome dress uniform, while Grant wore a mud-spattered field uniform. The contrast became one of the best-remembered details of the surrender meeting.

Politics & the War

Why this battle mattered beyond the battlefield

Only days after Appomattox, Lincoln was assassinated. That sudden shock meant that the nation entered Reconstruction without the president who had guided the Union through the war.

Why It Mattered

Appomattox signaled the collapse of major Confederate resistance. Grant allowed Confederate soldiers to return home with their horses and promised they would not be prosecuted for treason if they obeyed the law and the terms of surrender. The moment helped shape the memory of the war’s ending.

Appomattox = Arms are laid down. The war is not remembered for one last huge battle here, but for surrender.
