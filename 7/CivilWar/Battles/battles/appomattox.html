Before the Fighting

The Confederacy is collapsing

By spring 1865, the Confederate war effort was breaking apart. Richmond and Petersburg had fallen, Lee’s army was retreating westward, and Union forces were moving to cut off any escape. Lee hoped to reach supplies and perhaps join other Confederate forces, but the Union had become too strong and too fast.

Why Appomattox was more than one last skirmish

Appomattox is remembered as the symbolic ending of the war not because it featured the biggest final battle, but because it ended the story of the Army of Northern Virginia. That army had become the Confederacy’s most famous field force. Once it could no longer continue, the meaning of resistance changed for the rest of the Confederacy too.

Document to Know

Terms of Surrender

Grant’s terms were lenient. Confederate soldiers could go home if they obeyed the law and the surrender terms, and many were allowed to keep horses useful for spring planting.

How It Unfolded

1

Lee’s road is blocked

Grant defeated Lee through relentless pursuit and superior numbers. Union forces repeatedly moved ahead of Lee’s retreat, cut off routes toward supplies and other Confederate armies, and eventually surrounded his exhausted, hungry, badly outnumbered force near Appomattox.

2

Further fighting looks hopeless

On April 9, Lee recognized that continued resistance would only cause more suffering without changing the outcome. Military honor and human cost now pointed in the same direction: surrender.

3

Grant and Lee meet

The two generals met in the McLean House at Appomattox Court House. Lee surrendered the Army of Northern Virginia, creating one of the most famous scenes in American history.

Lee arrived in a handsome dress uniform, while Grant wore a mud-spattered field uniform. That contrast became one of the most remembered details of the surrender meeting.

What Happened After Lee Surrendered?

Other Confederate armies follow

Lee did not command every Confederate soldier, so the war did not end everywhere on April 9. But his surrender convinced many other Confederate leaders that further resistance was hopeless. Joseph E. Johnston surrendered a large army to Sherman on April 26, and other Confederate forces followed over the next several weeks.

Jefferson Davis is captured

Confederate President Jefferson Davis tried to escape after the Confederate government collapsed. Union troops captured him in Georgia on May 10, 1865. He was imprisoned at Fort Monroe in Virginia for about two years while the United States decided how to handle the former Confederate president.

Politics & the War

Why this mattered beyond the battlefield

Only days after Appomattox, Lincoln was assassinated. That shock meant the nation entered Reconstruction without the president who had guided the Union through the war, showing again how closely military and political history were linked.

Why It Mattered

Appomattox did not end every fight everywhere immediately, but it clearly signaled the collapse of major Confederate resistance. For students, it serves as the best symbolic endpoint to the military story of the Civil War.
