September–October 1781 • Virginia
“The Final Blow”

Yorktown

Yorktown was the last major battle of the American Revolution. American and French forces surrounded a British army led by General Cornwallis. With the French navy blocking escape by sea, the British were trapped—and forced to surrender.

Allies, planning, and control of the sea can decide a war—even more than a single battle.

Battle at a Glance

🗓️ Date: September 28–October 19, 1781
📍 Place: Yorktown, Virginia
👥 Who fought: American & French forces vs. British forces under Cornwallis
🎯 Allied goal: trap and force the British army to surrender
🏆 Outcome: British surrender; war moves toward peace

Background

By 1781, fighting had lasted for years. Britain still had strong forces, but the war was expensive and difficult to win. The United States had gained a crucial ally: France. French money, troops, and especially the French navy made a huge difference.

British General Charles Cornwallis moved his army to Yorktown, Virginia, near the coast. He expected the British navy to protect him and, if necessary, help him escape. American General George Washington and French leaders saw a chance to trap Cornwallis—if the French navy could block the sea.

The French victory at the Battle of the Chesapeake meant British ships could not reach Yorktown—Cornwallis was stuck.

Quick Facts

🚢 French ships blocked escape by sea.
🏰 Yorktown was attacked with a long siege.
🤝 Americans and French coordinated their forces.
🏳️ Cornwallis surrendered on October 19, 1781.

Vocabulary

siege — surrounding an area to cut off supplies and force surrender.
allies — partners who work together for a shared goal.
blockade — stopping ships or supplies from entering or leaving an area.
surrender — to give up to an enemy.
artillery — large guns such as cannons.
negotiation — talking to reach an agreement.

What Happened?

A trap forms

Washington and the French General Rochambeau marched their armies south toward Virginia. At the same time, a French fleet moved to control the waters near Yorktown. The plan was clear: surround Cornwallis on land and block him at sea.

The French navy blocks the coast

A key moment happened offshore. When the French navy controlled the Chesapeake area, British ships could not reach Yorktown to rescue Cornwallis. Without naval support, the British position became far more dangerous.

The siege and the cannons

American and French forces dug trenches and moved artillery into place. Day after day, cannons fired on British defenses. Over time, Cornwallis’s situation worsened as supplies ran low and defenses were damaged.

The surrender

With no easy way out, Cornwallis agreed to surrender on October 19, 1781. The loss was a major shock in Britain. Even though smaller battles continued, Yorktown was the last major battle—and it pushed Britain to negotiate peace.

Why Did Yorktown Matter?

Yorktown mattered because it effectively ended the war. Britain lost a large army, and many leaders in Britain began to believe the war was no longer worth the cost.

It also showed the power of alliances. American troops fought bravely, but French help—especially the navy—made the victory possible. After Yorktown, peace talks increased and eventually led to the Treaty of Paris (1783), which recognized American independence.
