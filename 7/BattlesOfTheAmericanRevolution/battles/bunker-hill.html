Bunker Hill

The Battle of Bunker Hill was one of the first major battles of the American Revolution. Although the British won control of the hill, Patriot forces proved they could stand up to the powerful British army in open battle.

Even a loss can become a turning point if it proves strength, courage, and determination.

Battle at a Glance

🗓️ Date: June 17, 1775
📍 Place: Charlestown, Massachusetts
👥 Who fought: British army vs. colonial militia
🎯 British goal: Break the siege of Boston
🏆 Outcome: British victory with heavy losses

Background

After Lexington and Concord, colonial forces surrounded Boston, trapping British troops inside the city. This was known as the siege of Boston.

Both sides understood that whoever controlled the high ground around Boston would have a major advantage. Patriot leaders decided to build defenses on nearby hills overlooking the city.

The famous phrase “Don’t fire until you see the whites of their eyes” may or may not have actually been said — but it shows how carefully Patriots had to conserve ammunition.

Quick Facts

⛰️ Most fighting happened on Breed’s Hill.
🔥 The town of Charlestown was set on fire.
🧍 Colonial militia held their ground twice.
💥 Patriots ran low on ammunition.
⚰️ British casualties were extremely high.

Vocabulary

siege — surrounding a city to cut off supplies.
redoubt — a small defensive fortification.
casualties — soldiers killed or wounded.
retreat — to pull back from fighting.
fortification — a defensive military structure.

What Happened?

Building defenses overnight

On the night of June 16, Patriot forces quietly built a redoubt on Breed’s Hill.

The British attack

The next day, British troops marched uphill in organized lines. Colonial militia waited until the enemy was close before firing. Twice, the British were forced to retreat.

A costly victory

On the third attack, the Patriots ran out of ammunition and had to retreat. The British captured the hill — but at a terrible cost. Over 1,000 British soldiers were killed or wounded.

Why Did Bunker Hill Matter?

Although the British won the battlefield, the victory was extremely expensive. The heavy British losses shocked leaders in London.

For the Patriots, the battle proved they could stand up to professional soldiers. Confidence and morale grew, even after retreating.
