“A Surprise in the North”

Fort Ticonderoga

Just weeks after Lexington and Concord, colonial fighters launched a daring surprise attack on a British fort in upstate New York. Their quick victory gave the Patriots much-needed artillery and a powerful boost of confidence.

Battle at a Glance

🗓️ Date: May 10, 1775
📍 Place: Fort Ticonderoga, New York
👥 Who fought: Green Mountain Boys & colonial militia vs. British garrison
🎯 Goal: Capture cannons and control the fort
🏆 Result: American victory

What Happened?

After fighting broke out in Massachusetts, Patriot leaders realized they would need heavy weapons to take on the British army. Fort Ticonderoga, located along Lake Champlain in New York, guarded an important water route between Canada and the colonies. More importantly, it stored dozens of valuable cannons.

A group of fighters known as the Green Mountain Boys, led by Ethan Allen, joined forces with Benedict Arnold to capture the fort. In the early morning hours of May 10, 1775, they crossed the lake quietly and approached the fort while most British soldiers were still asleep.

The British garrison was small and unprepared. The Patriots demanded surrender, and the surprised British commander gave up the fort without a fight. Not a single person was killed during the takeover.

The victory may have seemed small, but it gave the Patriots something they desperately needed: artillery. Months later, Henry Knox transported the captured cannons hundreds of miles to Boston. When those cannons were placed on high ground overlooking the city, the British were forced to leave.

Why It Matters

Fort Ticonderoga provided the Americans with powerful weapons at a critical moment in the war. It also boosted Patriot morale. After Lexington and Concord, colonists were unsure what would happen next. This quick and successful mission showed that colonial forces could defeat British troops through bold action and careful planning.

The captured cannons later helped drive British forces out of Boston, proving that even a bloodless victory could have huge consequences.

The cannons from Fort Ticonderoga were dragged nearly 300 miles through snow and ice by Henry Knox and his team during the winter of 1775–1776!

Vocabulary
