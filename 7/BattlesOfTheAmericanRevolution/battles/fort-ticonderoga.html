“A Surprise in the North”

Fort Ticonderoga

Fort Ticonderoga was a British fort guarding a key route between Canada and the colonies. In a quick early-morning raid, Patriot forces captured it—along with valuable artillery that would soon help force the British out of Boston.

Capturing the right resources at the right moment can turn a small victory into a major advantage.

Battle at a Glance

🗓️ Date: May 10, 1775
📍 Place: Fort Ticonderoga, New York
👥 Who fought: Patriots (including the Green Mountain Boys) vs. a small British garrison
🎯 Patriot goal: capture the fort and its cannons
🏆 Outcome: Fort captured quickly with little to no fighting

Background

After Lexington and Concord, Patriot leaders knew the conflict was growing. But there was a big problem: the colonies had plenty of brave militia members, but not enough heavy weapons. Fort Ticonderoga held cannons and controlled an important route along Lake Champlain, connecting the colonies to Canada.

Even though the fort was far from Boston, Patriots realized it could help them in two ways: it could keep the British from using the lake route, and it could provide supplies that the colonies desperately needed.

Fort Ticonderoga was captured so quickly that many British soldiers were still asleep—some Patriots joked it was “the quietest victory” of the early war.

What Happened?

Why Fort Ticonderoga mattered

Fort Ticonderoga sat near a narrow point between lakes and rivers, making it a natural “gateway” for travel and trade. In wartime, controlling that route could help block British movement from Canada—and the fort’s cannons could strengthen Patriot defenses.

The plan: strike before the British could react

Patriot leaders organized a surprise attack instead of a long siege. Fighters from the Green Mountain Boys (led by Ethan Allen) joined with Benedict Arnold. Their goal was simple: get in fast, take control, and capture the weapons.

The capture: a fort taken with almost no fighting

Before sunrise on May 10, the Patriots crossed the water and approached the fort quietly. The British garrison was small and unprepared. The commander was forced to surrender, and the fort fell into Patriot hands before the British could organize a defense.

The cannons: a “weapon upgrade” for the Patriots

The biggest prize was the artillery. Later, Henry Knox led the effort to move the captured cannons over hundreds of miles—through rough roads and winter conditions. Those cannons would eventually be used to pressure British forces in Boston, helping push them to leave the city.

Why Did Fort Ticonderoga Matter?

Fort Ticonderoga mattered because it gave the Patriots something they lacked: heavy guns. Early in the war, morale and momentum mattered a lot, and this victory boosted confidence after the uncertainty of the first fighting.

It also showed that Patriot forces could win through planning and speed—not just by reacting to British actions. A “quiet” victory in the north helped shape what happened next near Boston, where the captured cannons became a major threat.
