# Southeast image plan

All images remain intentional placeholders. Slot names match `data-image-slot` in each HTML page. Supply credited, historically identified images; use WEBP when integrating. Preserve aspect ratio, useful context, and full maps/documents.

## overview

- **map — Southeast culture-region map** (4:5): A map highlighting the Southeast on North America, with the southern Appalachians, major rivers, Gulf Coast, and Florida. Keep regional boundaries approximate.
- **food — A southeastern food system** (4:3): A historically grounded scene of cornfields beside a river town, or a clear teaching image showing corn, beans, squash, fishing, and deer. Avoid Plains bison imagery.
- **mounds — A mound town and central plaza** (4:3): A labeled reconstruction of a southeastern Mississippian town showing flat-topped mounds, a plaza, homes, and fields. Identify the actual site and time period.
- **moundville — Moundville Archaeological Park** (4:3): A wide or aerial view that shows several mounds and the central plaza. Look for a site plan if a photograph does not show the arrangement clearly.
- **cane — River cane and a finished basket** (4:3): A paired image of living river cane and a southeastern basket with the maker and nation identified. A labeled wattle-and-daub wall detail could complement it.
- **removal — Several removal routes** (4:3): A clear map of Cherokee, Muscogee, Choctaw, Chickasaw, and Seminole routes toward Indian Territory. Show Oklahoma’s modern location and distinguish land and water travel.
- **today — Native nations are here today** (4:3): A present-day southeastern Native language class, community event, or artist at work. Identify the actual nation, people if known, date, and photographer; avoid a generic powwow photograph.
- **hero — The Southeast: homeland and community** (16:10): A historically grounded southeastern river-town reconstruction with fields, woodland, and a central community space. Identify the nation or site and the time period.

## cherokee

- **map — Cherokee ancestral homelands** (4:5): A map of southern Appalachian Cherokee homelands with major rivers, New Echota, and the present-day Qualla Boundary marked. Distinguish historical territory from modern lands.
- **basket — Cherokee river-cane basket** (4:3): A clearly credited Cherokee river-cane basket, ideally with its maker identified, paired with a photograph of the maker or raw cane. Look at Qualla Arts and Crafts or a museum collection.
- **person — Sequoyah** (5:6.4): A documented historic portrait or painting of Sequoyah. Label an artwork as an artistic representation rather than a photograph; portrait format about 500 × 640 pixels.
- **newspaper — Cherokee Phoenix front page** (4:3): A legible 1828 issue showing both Cherokee syllabary and English columns. The Library of Congress has historical issues. Keep the whole page visible rather than cropping away the text.
- **removal — Cherokee removal routes, 1838–1839** (4:3): A map showing multiple land and water routes from the Cherokee homelands to Indian Territory. Choose a readable NPS map rather than a single arrow marked “1,000-mile walk.”
- **site — New Echota print shop or council house** (4:3): A credited photograph of New Echota’s reconstructed print shop or council house. Identify the reconstruction and its historical purpose.
- **today — Cherokee nations today** (4:3): A current Cherokee language classroom, artist, or community gathering; identify whether it shows Cherokee Nation, Eastern Band, or United Keetoowah Band. A syllabary sign could provide a useful detail.
- **hero — Cherokee: homeland and community** (16:10): A Cherokee town reconstruction in a southern Appalachian river valley, with the site and period identified. Avoid Plains tipis, feathered warbonnets, or a generic “Native village.”

## muscogee-creek

- **map — Muscogee river-town homelands** (4:5): A historical map of towns in Alabama and Georgia showing the four named rivers. Mark Ocmulgee near present-day Macon, and distinguish historical homelands from the modern Oklahoma nation.
- **town — A Muscogee town plan** (4:3): An identified reconstruction or plan showing homes, fields, a central square ground, and council spaces. Label the time period; do not substitute a Plains encampment.
- **food-house — Muscogee farming and house construction** (4:3): A historically identified Muscogee or ancestral town image showing fields and houses, or a clear wattle-and-daub reconstruction with the woven framework visible.
- **renewal — Corn, a square ground, or public cultural teaching** (4:3): Prefer a permitted public image of fresh corn, a town-square teaching diagram, or a Muscogee cultural educator. Do not seek private ceremonial photographs or staged “tribal rituals.”
- **horseshoe-bend — Horseshoe Bend and Tohopeka** (4:3): A clear battle-location map or aerial view showing the Tallapoosa River’s bend and the defended community of Tohopeka. Keep the focus on land, community, and the consequences of war.
- **person — Menawa** (5:6.4): A documented portrait or historic painting of Menawa, with creator and date when available. Identify it as artwork. Do not substitute a portrait of another Creek leader.
- **ocmulgee — Ocmulgee Mounds and earth lodge** (4:3): A landscape photo of Ocmulgee’s Great Temple Mound or a documented view of the reconstructed earth lodge. Identify the reconstruction and its surviving original floor when relevant.
- **today — Muscogee communities today** (4:3): A present-day Muscogee language class, College of the Muscogee Nation activity, or public return gathering at Ocmulgee. Identify the actual community and event.
- **hero — Muscogee (Creek): homeland and community** (16:10): A historically identified Muscogee river town, showing fields, homes, and a public square, or a wide view of an ancestral Ocmulgee landscape.

## seminole

- **map — Seminole homelands in Florida** (4:5): A map showing northern Florida town areas as well as later southern Florida refuges and the Everglades. Add an Oklahoma locator to show where many families were removed.
- **landscape — Everglades waterways and hammocks** (4:3): A clear landscape showing shallow waterways and raised wooded hammocks. A northern Florida farm or cattle image can show the wider history; avoid presenting all Seminole life as swamp life.
- **chickee — Seminole chickee construction** (4:3): A credited Seminole-built chickee showing the cypress frame, palmetto-thatch roof, open sides, and platform. A labeled diagram is useful; avoid a generic resort “tiki hut.”
- **black-seminoles — Black Seminole history** (4:3): A documented historic image of a named Black Seminole person or community, or a clear migration map linking Florida, Indian Territory, Mexico, and Texas. Do not use an unidentified portrait.
- **person — Abiaka (Sam Jones)** (5:6.4): A documented historical representation of Abiaka, or a photographed memorial identified by name. Be careful: online portraits are sometimes mislabeled or substituted with Osceola.
- **patchwork — Seminole patchwork and its maker** (4:3): A credited Seminole maker sewing or wearing identified patchwork, plus a detail of the geometric bands if possible. Do not describe the early-1900s style as ancient clothing.
- **museum — Ah-Tah-Thi-Ki Museum or boardwalk** (4:3): A credited exterior, public exhibit, or boardwalk photograph from Ah-Tah-Thi-Ki Museum. Respect museum photography rules and identify what is shown.
- **today — Seminole and Miccosukee communities today** (4:3): A current Seminole language program, patchwork workshop, ranching scene, or community event. Name the tribe or community and do not confuse Seminole and Miccosukee identities.
- **hero — Seminole: homeland and community** (16:10): A historically grounded Seminole community scene with chickees and a clearly identified period and location, or a wide Florida homeland landscape.
