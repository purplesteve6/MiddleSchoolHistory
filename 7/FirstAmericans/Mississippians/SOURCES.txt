WEB SOURCES
-----------
Lumen Learning — “Mississippian Culture”
https://courses.lumenlearning.com/suny-hccc-worldcivilization/chapter/mississippian-culture/
Used especially for the original Part 1 scope: Three Sisters, mounds, farming, palisades, trade networks, art, and broad Mississippian cultural traits.

National Park Service — “Mississippian Culture at Russell Cave”
https://www.nps.gov/ruca/learn/historyculture/mississippian-culture.htm
Used for Mississippian agriculture, permanent communities, trade, and Three Sisters context.

National Park Service — “History and Culture of the Mississippi Delta Region”
https://www.nps.gov/locations/lowermsdeltaregion/history-and-culture-of-the-mississippi-delta-region.htm
Used for the broad definition of Mississippian societies, river-valley geography, agriculture, and changes during/after European contact.

National Park Service — “Archeology This Month: Native American Heritage”
https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/archeology-this-month-native-american-heritage.htm
Used for Cahokia population estimates, approximately 120 mounds, and Monks Mound height.

National Park Service — “Midwest Region's Only World Heritage Site...and a Promise of More to Come”
https://www.nps.gov/articles/midwest-region-s-only-world-heritage-site-and-a-promise-of-more-to-come.htm
Used for Cahokia’s peak dates, size, population range, mound count, and abandonment by around 1400.

Cahokia Mounds State Historic Site
https://cahokiamounds.org/
Used for current site facts, World Heritage status, protected acreage, and surviving mound counts.

Cahokia Mounds State Historic Site — “Explore”
https://cahokiamounds.org/explore/
Used for the stockade and Woodhenge descriptions.

Cahokia Mounds State Historic Site — Site Brochure
https://cahokiamounds.org/wpress/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/Site-Brochure-8-13.pdf
Used for the stockade, Woodhenge, houses, Monks Mound, and mound-function context.

National Park Service — Cahokia Mounds Reconnaissance Survey
https://parkplanning.nps.gov/showFile.cfm?projectID=66149&sfid=381753
Used for descendant/continuity language and for the effects of modern development on Greater Cahokia/St. Louis mound groups.

Smithsonian Magazine — “Why Did Cahokia, One of North America’s Largest Pre-Hispanic Cities, Collapse?”
https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/why-did-cahokia-one-largest-pre-hispanic-cities-north-america-collapse-180977528/
Used to keep the decline discussion appropriately uncertain and multi-causal rather than presenting one unproven explanation as fact.
