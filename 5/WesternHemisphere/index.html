Grade 5 Geography

The Western Hemisphere: Four Regions

The Western Hemisphere stretches from the frozen Arctic to the southern tip of South America. It includes enormous countries, tropical islands, towering mountains, wide plains, and some of the world's largest rivers.

To make this huge part of the world easier to study, we will organize it into four regions. These four regions will help us understand where places are located and how they fit together throughout Grade 5 Social Studies.

01
Start Here

What Is the Western Hemisphere?

Earth is shaped roughly like a sphere. A hemisphere is one half of that sphere. Geographers use imaginary lines around Earth to help divide it into hemispheres.

The Equator

The Equator circles Earth halfway between the North Pole and the South Pole. It divides Earth into the Northern Hemisphere and the Southern Hemisphere.

The Prime Meridian

The Prime Meridian runs from the North Pole to the South Pole at 0° longitude. Together with the line on the opposite side of Earth, it helps geographers divide Earth into the Eastern Hemisphere and the Western Hemisphere.

Most of North America and South America are located in the Western Hemisphere. That is why the places we study in Grade 5 are often described together as part of the Western Hemisphere.

Continents and Regions

A continent is one of Earth's largest land areas. North America and South America are continents.

A region is a way of organizing places that share something in common. Regions might be based on location, landforms, climate, history, culture, or other characteristics.

Continent or Region?

These words do not always mean the same thing.

For example, Mexico is geographically located on the continent of North America. However, in our Grade 5 Social Studies course, we will use the regional organization found in the New York State framework. In that system, Mexico is grouped with Central America in a region called Mesoamerica.

So both statements can be true: Mexico is on the North American continent, and Mexico is part of our Mesoamerica region.

Our Four Regions

Throughout this course, we will organize the Western Hemisphere into four major regions:

  1. North America — Canada and the United States
  2. Mesoamerica — Mexico and Central America
  3. The Caribbean
  4. South America

Now let's travel through those four regions one at a time.

02
Region One

North America

For our Grade 5 course, the North America region consists of Canada and the United States. Together, these two countries cover an enormous area stretching from the Arctic to the warm waters farther south.

Three Oceans

This region touches three of the world's major oceans. The Atlantic Ocean lies to the east, the Pacific Ocean lies to the west, and the Arctic Ocean lies to the north.

Mountains from East to West

Two major mountain systems stretch across large parts of this region. The Rocky Mountains rise through western North America, while the older Appalachian Mountains stretch through eastern parts of the United States and Canada.

The Great Plains

Between major mountain areas lies a huge region of mostly flat or gently rolling land called the Great Plains. The Great Plains stretch through the center of the United States and into Canada.

The Great Lakes

Along part of the border between the United States and Canada lies a group of five enormous freshwater lakes known as the Great Lakes. They are Lakes Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie, and Ontario.

Together, the Great Lakes form one of the largest systems of fresh surface water on Earth.

Major Rivers

Rivers have helped shape where people live, travel, farm, and trade. The Mississippi River is one of the most important rivers in the United States. It flows south through the center of the country toward the Gulf of Mexico.

Farther north and east, the St. Lawrence River connects the Great Lakes region with the Atlantic Ocean and has long served as an important transportation route.

03
Region Two

Mesoamerica

Our Mesoamerica region begins with Mexico in the north and continues south through the countries of Central America.

The Countries of Central America

Central America contains seven countries: Belize, Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, and Panama.

A Narrow Land Bridge

Central America is an example of an isthmus. An isthmus is a narrow strip of land connecting two larger land areas, with water on either side.

The Pacific Ocean lies along the western side of Central America, while the Caribbean Sea lies along much of its eastern side.

Panama: The Link to South America

At the southern end of Central America is Panama. Panama connects Central America with the continent of South America.

The Panama Canal

Panama is also home to one of the world's most famous human-made waterways: the Panama Canal. The canal cuts across Panama and allows ships to travel between the Atlantic and Pacific sides without sailing all the way around South America.

Looking Ahead: The Maya

Long before modern national borders existed, one of the great civilizations of the Western Hemisphere developed in parts of Mexico and Central America. The Maya built cities, developed writing and mathematics, and carefully studied the movement of the Sun, Moon, and stars.

We will meet the Maya again later in Grade 5.

04
Region Three

The Caribbean

Between North America and South America lies the Caribbean Sea. The many islands in and around this sea form the region we call the Caribbean.

A Region of Islands

Unlike the large connected land areas of Canada, the United States, or South America, the Caribbean is made up mainly of islands. A large group or chain of islands can be called an archipelago.

There are many Caribbean islands, and you will not need to memorize every one. Some of the most important islands for our course are Cuba, Hispaniola, Jamaica, and Puerto Rico.

One Island, Two Countries

The island of Hispaniola is shared by two different countries. Haiti occupies the western part of the island, while the Dominican Republic occupies the eastern part.

Puerto Rico and the United States

Puerto Rico is not one of the fifty U.S. states. It is a territory of the United States.

People born in Puerto Rico are U.S. citizens, but Puerto Rico has a different political status from a state such as New York or Florida.

05
Region Four

South America

South America stretches from the warm Caribbean coast in the north toward the cold waters near Antarctica in the south. Its landscape includes enormous mountains, rivers, forests, grasslands, deserts, and coastlines.

The Andes Mountains

The Andes Mountains form a long chain along the western side of South America. They stretch for thousands of miles and are one of the world's longest mountain systems.

The Amazon River

Northern South America is also home to the enormous Amazon River. The Amazon and its many tributaries carry tremendous amounts of water across the continent toward the Atlantic Ocean.

The Amazon Basin

The Amazon Basin is the huge area of land drained by the Amazon River and its tributaries. Much of this basin is covered by the Amazon Rainforest, the largest tropical rainforest on Earth.

Brazil: South America's Largest Country

Brazil is the largest country in South America. It covers a huge part of the continent and includes much of the Amazon Basin.

Spanish and Portuguese

Language is another important feature of South American geography. Portuguese is the main language spoken in Brazil.

Spanish is the main language spoken in most of the other countries of South America.

Countries of the Andes

Because the Andes stretch along so much of western South America, the mountains are connected with several different countries. From north to south, the Andes pass through or along Venezuela, Colombia, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Chile, and Argentina.

06
Put It Together

Four Regions, One Hemisphere

The Western Hemisphere is enormous, but organizing it into regions makes its geography easier to understand.

North America

Canada + United States

Think: Rockies, Great Plains, Great Lakes, Mississippi River

Mesoamerica

Mexico + Central America

Think: isthmus, seven Central American countries, Panama Canal

The Caribbean

Caribbean Sea + islands

Think: Cuba, Hispaniola, Jamaica, Puerto Rico

South America

The South American continent

Think: Andes, Amazon, Brazil

Big Idea

A region is a tool geographers use to organize the world. The borders of a region depend on what we are trying to study. Throughout Grade 5, these four regions will help us keep track of where people, civilizations, countries, and events belong.
